The number of starving and ownerless Dogs (1) had
increased to such an extent that they were looked on
as a plague (dogs were not licensed till 1885), and during
the ever-recurring periods of famine and fever which filled
the graveyards, these pests tore open the shallow graves
of those days and fed on the dead bodies, and their
depredations increased to such an extent that armed
guards had to be placed round the graveyards.
